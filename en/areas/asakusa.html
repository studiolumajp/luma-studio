Sho's note

Kaminarimon

The lantern is 3.9 metres tall and weighs about 700 kg. Konosuke Matsushita paid for the gate in 1960, as thanks for getting over his joint pain.

Sho's note

Hozomon

Look up at the straw sandals: 4.5 metres tall and 500 kg, given by Murayama in Yamagata. They're meant to scare off evil by hinting at the size of the gate's guardians.

Sho's note

Kappabashi

One story says kappa from the Sumida River helped a man named Kihachi dig a canal here. Today it's a street of shops selling tools for cooking and food.

The area around Senso-ji is flat and easy on foot. The free 8th-floor terrace of the tourist centre opposite Kaminarimon shows the whole route at once. You can see Nakamise running up to Hozomon, the main hall, and the Skytree across the river.

Nakamise dates to around 1685. Its paving stones were given in 1817 by a local association, because the approach to the temple used to turn to mud.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Is there an entry fee at Senso-ji?

No fee is listed for Senso-ji or Asakusa Shrine. The main hall opens at 6 am (6:30 am from October to March).

Can we go up the Skytree?

Yes, if you'd like. Tickets start at 1,800 yen and vary by day, and buying at the counter on the day adds 500 yen a ticket.
