For you

For groups that need a little more planning

This page is for you if:

  • Someone in your group has coeliac disease, a food allergy or another dietary need.
  • You're travelling with young children who'll need breaks along the way.
  • An older parent is coming along and would like a gentler pace.
  • Long walks or lots of stairs are hard for someone in the group.
  • You'd like restaurants checked before you land.
How I plan it

What I did for a gluten-free food walk

Here's how I planned a gluten-free food walk in Tokyo on 8 November 2026 for two guests.

01
Step 01

Ask first

Before the tour, I asked the guests about their dietary needs and the food they like.

02
Step 02

Check official information

I read each restaurant's official information, such as its website or booking page. I looked for gluten-free certification and any statement about cross-contamination.

03
Step 03

Leave lunch open

I didn't book lunch. I listed three options in the itinerary, and we chose one together on the day.

04
Step 04

Print the itinerary

I handed the guests a printed itinerary with the route, the address of each stop and what to bring.

05
Step 05

Write a guide for the next stops

After the tour, I put together a guide to their next stops: Kanazawa, Kyoto and Osaka.

Food and allergies

I check what I can, and you decide

Before the day, I read each restaurant's official information. When we get there, I ask the staff how the dish is prepared.

Even so, I can't guarantee there's no cross-contamination in any kitchen. What you eat is always your decision.

Bookings and transport

I guide. I don't book or drive.

I plan the day and guide you through it. I don't book trains, hotels, entry tickets or restaurant tables for you.

We get around by train, bus and on foot, and I don't drive guests. If walking is hard for someone, I plan shorter stretches and rest stops, and choose routes with fewer stairs where I can.

Pricing
