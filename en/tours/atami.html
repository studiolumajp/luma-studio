Atami, from the old camphor tree down to the sea

A hot-spring town on the water, where famous writers once stayed and the old geyser now erupts about every five minutes. Up to six people, by bus and on foot.

Overview

A hot-spring town with long memories

Atami Station opens straight onto two shopping streets, Nakamise and Heiwa-dori, where some shops have been trading for 60 to 70 years. Dried fish, sushi and onsen manju are the things to look for.

Fifteen minutes away by bus, Kinomiya Shrine has a camphor tree more than 2,100 years old, 24 metres around the trunk. Local lore says one lap of it adds a year to your life.

Kiunkaku was built as a private villa in 1919 and became an inn in 1947. Dazai Osamu and Tanizaki Jun'ichiro both stayed there, and today you can walk through the rooms and the garden.

Highlights

  • Walk a lap of a camphor tree more than 2,100 years old
  • Tour Kiunkaku, a 1919 villa where well-known writers stayed
  • Watch the Oyu geyser, which now erupts about every five minutes
  • Pick out dried fish on the shopping streets by the station
  • Visit the MOA Museum of Art, whose collection includes National Treasures (full day)
Routes

Pick the length that suits your day

2 hours

The station streets, the geyser and the seafront, on foot.

  1. Station shopping streetsNakamise and Heiwa-dori, for dried fish and onsen manju.
  2. Oyu Geyserabout 20 minutes' walk. It stopped in 1923 and now erupts artificially about every five minutes.
  3. Omiya no Matsuthe pine and statue from the novel Konjiki Yasha, beside a jacaranda path that flowers in May and June.
  4. Atami Sun Beachthen about five minutes by bus back to the station.

Half day (3 to 4 hours)

Up to the shrine by bus, then down through the villa to the sea.

  1. Kinomiya Shrineabout 15 minutes by bus, for a lap of the giant camphor tree.
  2. Kiunkakuthe 1919 villa, about 10 minutes by bus from the station.
  3. Omiya no Matsu and Sun Beacha walk down to the water.
  4. Station shopping streetsdried fish and Atami pudding before you head off.

Full day (7 to 8 hours)

Art in the morning, the shrine and villa after lunch, the beach at dusk.

  1. MOA Museum of Artabout 7 minutes by bus from the station, with around two hours inside.
  2. Luncha dried-fish set meal near the station.
  3. Kinomiya Shrine and Kiunkakuby bus, as on the half day.
  4. Oyu Geyser, Omiya no Matsu and Sun Beachthe beach is lit up from sunset to 10 pm.

The Sun Beach lighting was designed by Motoko Ishii, and it was the first beach in Japan to be lit up this way.

Two days

Atami on its own doesn't fill two days, so I pair it with Ito, further down the Izu coast.

  1. Day 1the Atami full day.
  2. Day 2JR Ito Line to Ito for the Ito full day: Tokaikan, the William Adams story and Mt Omuro.

Good to know

  • I use the bus for the stops up the hill, the shrine and the art museum, so most of our walking is on the way down to the sea.
  • Kiunkaku closes on Wednesdays and the MOA Museum on Thursdays (both open on public holidays), so I plan around them.
  • Rain plan: the MOA Museum and Kiunkaku keep us indoors for most of the day.
  • Onsen manju fillings differ from shop to shop, so I check before we buy.
  • I look after the day itself. Train tickets, hotels and any entry tickets you want to book ahead are yours to arrange. I'll tell you exactly where and when to meet me.
  • We get around by train, bus and on foot, the same way people here do. I don't drive guests, and I'll show you how the ticket machines work as we go.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Does a lap of the camphor tree really add a year?

That's the local story. There's a second one: walk around it while keeping a wish to yourself, and it'll come true.

Is the geyser natural?

Not any more. It stopped erupting on its own in 1923, was kept as a city cultural property from 1962, and now erupts artificially about every five minutes.
