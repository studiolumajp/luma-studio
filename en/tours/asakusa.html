Asakusa, from the Thunder Gate to Kappabashi

A temple with nearly 1,400 years of history, on foot, with a detour to a street of kitchen-tool shops. Up to six people.

Overview

Tokyo's old downtown, gate by gate

Senso-ji's own records say its image of Kannon appeared in 628, which gives the temple nearly 1,400 years of history. We walk in the traditional way: through Kaminarimon, down Nakamise and under Hozomon to the main hall.

Kaminarimon's official name is Furaijinmon. It burned down in 1865 and stood empty for 95 years until Konosuke Matsushita paid for a new one in 1960, as thanks for getting over his joint pain. The red lantern is 3.9 metres tall and weighs about 700 kg.

Nakamise runs about 250 metres from the gate to Hozomon and dates back to around 1685. It's one of the oldest shopping streets in Japan. The straw sandals on Hozomon are 4.5 metres tall, hung there to tell evil spirits how big the gate's guardians must be.

Highlights

  • Walk under Kaminarimon's 700 kg lantern
  • Shop your way along Nakamise, a street dating to around 1685
  • Visit Senso-ji and Asakusa Shrine, whose 1649 building is an Important Cultural Property
  • See the whole district from the free 8th-floor terrace of the tourist centre
  • Browse the kitchenware shops of Kappabashi (half and full day)
Routes

Pick the length that suits your day

2 hours

Senso-ji and its gates, on foot, about 1.5 km in all.

  1. Tourist centre terracea free look over Asakusa and the Skytree from the 8th floor.
  2. Kaminarimonthe gate and its giant lantern.
  3. Nakamiseabout 250 metres of shops, with a snack or two on the way.
  4. Hozomonthe gate with the 4.5-metre straw sandals.
  5. Senso-ji main hallopen from 6 am (6:30 am from October to March).
  6. Asakusa Shrinebuilt in 1649 with a donation from Tokugawa Iemitsu.

We finish back near Kaminarimon.

Half day (3 to 4 hours)

The temple, then Kappabashi and across the Sumida River.

  1. Senso-ji and Asakusa Shrineas on the 2-hour tour.
  2. Kappabashia street of kitchen and restaurant supply shops that started with a handful of dealers in 1912.
  3. Sumida River Walka footbridge beside the Tobu railway bridge, over to Tokyo Mizumachi.
  4. Tokyo Skytree, if you'd like to go up634 metres tall, with decks at 350 and 450 metres.

About 4 km of walking in total. Skytree tickets start at 1,800 yen and vary by day.

Full day (7 to 8 hours)

Asakusa in the morning, Ueno's national museum in the afternoon.

  1. Senso-ji, Nakamise and Asakusa Shrineabout two hours.
  2. Lunch in Asakusa.
  3. Kappabashithen the Ginza Line from Tawaramachi to Ueno.
  4. Tokyo National Museum10 to 15 minutes' walk from Ueno Station, about two hours inside.

Another option for the afternoon: the Tokyo Cruise water bus from Asakusa to Hinode Pier, about 40 minutes.

Two days

Asakusa alone is a lot for two days, so I pair it with Ryogoku and another Tokyo area.

  1. Day 1the Asakusa full day.
  2. Day 2the Edo-Tokyo Museum in Ryogoku, reopened in 2026, then a half day in Shibuya or Shinjuku.

Good to know

  • Visiting Senso-ji and Asakusa Shrine is free.
  • The Tokyo National Museum and the Edo-Tokyo Museum close on Mondays. The national museum says the ticket queue can take over 30 minutes at busy times, so buy online.
  • Kaminari okoshi, the local rice cracker, comes in a caramel almond flavour. Almond is a rose-family food, so I leave that one out.
  • Rain plan: the two museums, and the rest area on the 6th floor of the tourist centre.
  • I look after the day itself. Train tickets, hotels and any entry tickets you want to book ahead are yours to arrange. I'll tell you exactly where and when to meet me.
  • We get around by train, bus and on foot, the same way people here do. I don't drive guests, and I'll show you how the ticket machines work as we go.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Why is it called the Thunder Gate?

Its full name, Furaijinmon, means the gate of the wind and thunder gods. No one knows exactly when people shortened it to Kaminarimon, but comic poems from 1804 to 1818 already use that name.

Is Asakusa hilly?

The area around Senso-ji is flat, and the 2-hour route is about 1.5 km on foot.
