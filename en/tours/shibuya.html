Overview

Crowds, then a forest

Hachi was an Akita dog born in Odate in 1923. His owner, Professor Ueno, died suddenly in 1925, and Hachi kept waiting for him at Shibuya Station. The first statue went up in 1934 with Hachi himself at the unveiling. The one you see now was unveiled on 15 August 1948.

A few steps away is the scramble crossing, where Tokyo's own tourism site says more than 1,000 people cross at a time.

Meiji Jingu, founded in 1920, sits in about 70 hectares of forest. It isn't a natural forest. Around 100,000 trees were donated from all over Japan and planted when the shrine was built.

Routes

Pick the length that suits your day

2 hours

Shibuya on foot: Hachiko, the crossing, a rooftop park.

  1. Hachiko statuethe story of the dog who waited.
  2. Scramble crossingwe cross it, then watch it from the side.
  3. Miyashita Parka public park on a shopping building's roof, with a lawn of about 1,000 square metres.
  4. SHIBUYA SKY, if you'd likea rooftop deck at 229 metres, with timed tickets you book ahead.

We finish at Shibuya Station.

Half day (3 to 4 hours)

Meiji Jingu and Harajuku, then down to Shibuya.

  1. Meiji Jinguabout 10 minutes' walk from each entrance to the main shrine.
  2. Takeshita Streetright outside the station's Takeshita Exit, known for take-away crepes.
  3. Omotesando to Shibuyaon foot, or one stop on the JR line if your legs have had enough.
  4. Scramble crossing and Hachikoto finish.

Full day (7 to 8 hours)

Shrine, park and Harajuku in the morning, Shibuya into the evening.

  1. Meiji Jingu and the Meiji Jingu Museum (closed on Thursdays).
  2. Yoyogi Parkthree minutes from Harajuku Station, once an army ground, then US military housing, then the 1964 Olympic village.
  3. Lunch around Takeshita Street and Omotesando.
  4. Miyashita Park, the scramble crossing and SHIBUYA SKYevening entry costs more.

We finish at Shibuya Station.

Good to know

  • Meiji Jingu's gates follow sunrise and sunset, so opening hours change every month (in October, 5:40 am to 4:40 pm).
  • SHIBUYA SKY doesn't allow bags on the roof, and you may be turned away if you're late for your time slot. When it sells out online, it's sold out at the counter too.
  • At Takeshita Street I pick crepes with strawberry, chocolate or banana. Tell me what you can't eat and I'll check the fillings before we order.
  • Rain plan: the Meiji Jingu Museum and the indoor SKY GALLERY on the 46th floor of SHIBUYA SKY.
  • I look after the day itself. Train tickets, hotels and any entry tickets you want to book ahead are yours to arrange. I'll tell you exactly where and when to meet me.
  • We get around by train, bus and on foot, the same way people here do. I don't drive guests, and I'll show you how the ticket machines work as we go.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Is the Hachiko story true?

Yes. The Shibuya city library's records say Hachi waited at the station after Professor Ueno died in 1925, and Hachi was at the unveiling of his first statue in 1934.

Do we visit the bars in Shibuya?

No. I don't run drinking tours. The bar alley at Miyashita Park isn't on my routes.
