Overview

Fukuoka, Dazaifu and a day in Yufuin

The Hakata tours start at Gion Station, a minute from Tocho-ji. The temple is said to have been founded in 806 by Kobo Daishi on his return from China. Its Buddha is 10.8 metres tall and took from 1988 to 1992 to carve.

Six minutes' walk from Gion Station, Kushida Shrine has a decorated Yamakasa festival float about 13 metres high on display all year except June. There's a ginkgo tree there said to be 1,000 years old.

In Dazaifu, Tenmangu is five minutes' walk from the station and honours Sugawara no Michizane. On the approach we eat umegae mochi. Despite the name, there's no plum in it, just rice-cake dough around sweet red bean paste.

Yufuin is a day trip from Hakata. The Yufuin no Mori limited express takes about 2 hours 14 minutes from Hakata. From JR Yufuin Station it's about 10 minutes' walk to Yunotsubo Kaido, the main street. It runs for about 800 metres past shops, cafes, museums and workshops, with Mt Yufu in view. Kinrinko Lake is at the far end.

Highlights

  • See the 10.8-metre Fukuoka Daibutsu at Tocho-ji
  • Stand next to a 13-metre Yamakasa float at Kushida Shrine
  • Pick up some Hakata dialect at the Hakata Machiya Folk Museum
  • Walk to Dazaifu Tenmangu and eat umegae mochi on the way
  • Visit the Kyushu National Museum, Japan's fourth national museum
  • Take the Yufuin no Mori to Yufuin and walk Yunotsubo Kaido to Kinrinko Lake

Half day (3 to 4 hours)

Dazaifu: the shrine and the national museum.

  1. Umegae mochi on the approach150 yen each, from more than 30 shops.
  2. Dazaifu Tenmangufive minutes from Dazaifu Station.
  3. Kyushu National Museum10 minutes' walk, 60 to 90 minutes inside.

Full day (7 to 8 hours)

Hakata in the morning and Dazaifu in the afternoon, or a day trip to Yufuin.

  1. HakataTocho-ji, Kushida Shrine and the Hakata Machiya Folk Museum.
  2. Subway to Tenjin, then the Nishitetsu line out to Dazaifu.
  3. Dazaifu Tenmangu, umegae mochi and the Kyushu National Museum.

If you'd rather stay in the city, the afternoon can go to Ohori Park and the Fukuoka Castle ruins in Maizuru Park. The path around Ohori Park's pond is about 2 km.

For Yufuin, we take the Yufuin no Mori from Hakata (about 2 hours 14 minutes), then walk Yunotsubo Kaido to Kinrinko Lake. The Yufuin-go bus from Tenjin is the other option and takes about 2 hours 25 minutes.

Two days

Fukuoka on day one, Yufuin on day two.

  1. Day 1Hakata and Dazaifu, or Hakata with Ohori Park and Maizuru Park. A yatai street stall for dinner.
  2. Day 2The Yufuin no Mori from Hakata, then Yunotsubo Kaido and Kinrinko Lake.

Fukuoka had 103 licensed yatai at the end of January 2024, about 90% of all the street stalls in Japan. We go for the food.

Good to know

  • The Kyushu National Museum closes on Mondays (or the next day, if Monday is a public holiday).
  • Rain plan: the Kyushu National Museum, the Hakata Machiya Folk Museum and the Buddha hall at Tocho-ji.
  • On the 25th of each month, some shops also sell umegae mochi made with yomogi (mugwort).
  • Every seat on the Yufuin no Mori is reserved, and tickets go on sale at 10 am one month before departure. The train doesn't run every day, so check the timetable for your date before you book. The Yufuin-go bus also needs a reservation.
  • I look after the day itself. Train tickets, hotels and any entry tickets you want to book ahead are yours to arrange. I'll tell you exactly where and when to meet me.
  • We get around by train, bus and on foot, the same way people here do. I don't drive guests, and I'll show you how the ticket machines work as we go.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Is there really no plum in umegae mochi?

None. The name comes from a story: after Michizane was sent to Dazaifu, an old woman brought him mochi skewered on a plum branch.

Is the yatai dinner a drinking tour?

No. We go to a yatai for the food, not a bar crawl.

Can we fit Yufuin into a half day?

No. The train from Hakata takes about 2 hours 14 minutes, so Yufuin needs a full day, or the second day of a two-day tour.

Do you book the Yufuin no Mori for us?

No, you buy your own tickets. Seats go on sale at 10 am one month before departure, and I'll tell you which train we're taking once we've planned the day.
