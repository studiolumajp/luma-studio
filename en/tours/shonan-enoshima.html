Enoshima, over the bridge and round the island

A small island off the Shonan coast with shrines, a lighthouse deck and sea caves. Up to six people, on foot, by outdoor escalator and Enoden train.

Overview

An island of shrines and caves

From Katase-Enoshima Station it's 12 minutes on foot to the bronze torii at the start of the island, rebuilt in 1821. The street behind it climbs to Hetsumiya, part of Enoshima Shrine, founded in 1206 by Minamoto no Sanetomo.

The island is steep. The Enoshima Escar, Japan's first outdoor escalator (1959), lifts you 46 metres in about four minutes. At the top is the Samuel Cocking Garden, begun in 1882 by a British trader, and the Sea Candle lighthouse deck, with Mt Fuji on clear days.

On the far side, the Iwaya caves are where Enoshima's worship began. Local lore says the first cave, 152 metres deep, leads all the way to an ice cave at Mt Fuji.

Highlights

Good to know

  • Enoshima is hilly, with a lot of steps on the far side. Tell me if anyone in your group finds stairs hard.
  • Local shirasu is sold from 11 March to 31 December. Fishing stops from 1 January to 10 March, so there's no raw shirasu then.
  • Black kites sometimes swoop on food outdoors along this coast, so we eat somewhere sheltered.
  • The island is mostly outdoors and steep, so it's not a good rain-day choice. I'll suggest Kamakura or Yokohama instead.
  • I look after the day itself. Train tickets, hotels and any entry tickets you want to book ahead are yours to arrange. I'll tell you exactly where and when to meet me.
  • We get around by train, bus and on foot, the same way people here do. I don't drive guests, and I'll show you how the ticket machines work as we go.

FAQ

Questions people ask

What is the turtle at Okutsumiya?

A painting on the ceiling of the worship hall that seems to look straight at you wherever you stand. The original was by the Edo-period painter Sakai Hoitsu, and what's there now is a copy.

Can we see Mt Fuji?

On clear days, from the Sea Candle. I can't promise the weather.
