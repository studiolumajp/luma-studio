Kamakura, from the Great Buddha to the bamboo garden

A bronze Buddha begun in 1252, one of Japan's largest wooden statues, and green tea in a bamboo grove. Up to six people, by Enoden train, bus and on foot.

Overview

Shrines, temples and a bronze Buddha

Tsurugaoka Hachimangu began when Minamoto no Yoriyoshi brought the god of Iwashimizu Hachimangu from Kyoto to the shore at Yuigahama. His descendant Yoritomo moved it to its present site in 1180. The approach, Wakamiya-oji, runs about 2 km in a straight line from the beach.

The Great Buddha at Kotoku-in is a National Treasure, a bronze Amida Buddha. Work on it began in 1252. For 50 yen extra you can step inside it.

Hase-dera's Kannon stands 9.18 metres tall, one of the largest wooden statues in Japan. The temple dates its founding to 736.

Highlights

  • See Hase-dera's 9.18-metre wooden Kannon
  • Climb the great stone steps of Tsurugaoka Hachimangu
  • Drink matcha in the bamboo garden at Hokoku-ji (full day)
  • Ride the Enoden line between Kamakura and Enoshima

Good to know

  • Kamakura asks visitors not to eat while walking in crowded places. We eat in shops or where we can stop, and around the station, black kites sometimes snatch food from people's hands.
  • Inside the Great Buddha it gets hot and humid, and entry can stop on very hot days.
  • Most of this route is outdoors. In heavy rain I'll suggest switching to Yokohama's indoor stops.
  • I look after the day itself. Train tickets, hotels and any entry tickets you want to book ahead are yours to arrange. I'll tell you exactly where and when to meet me.
  • We get around by train, bus and on foot, the same way people here do. I don't drive guests, and I'll show you how the ticket machines work as we go.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Why is Kamakura's famous biscuit shaped like a dove?

The character for eight on the plaque of Tsurugaoka Hachimangu's main hall is drawn as two doves. Toshimaya's Hato Sable takes its shape from that.

Is there a lot of climbing?

The main shrine at Tsurugaoka Hachimangu sits at the top of a big stone staircase. The Great Buddha is on flat ground.
