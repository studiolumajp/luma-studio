Overview

A city built by its own people

Osaka Castle's main tower was rebuilt in 1931, 266 years after the last one, and the people of Osaka paid for it with their own donations. Inside, a lift takes you straight up to the fifth floor.

Dotonbori is named after Yasui Doton, who started digging the canal with his own money in 1612. You can walk right along the water on the Tombori River Walk.

Takoyaki goes back to early Showa-era Osaka, when street stalls began putting octopus into what they called radio-yaki. Okonomiyaki grew out of yoshoku-yaki from the late Meiji era, and in the 1960s and 70s chain shops sold it as Osaka's own dish.

Highlights

  • Take the lift up Osaka Castle's main tower, rebuilt by public donation in 1931
  • Walk the Dotonbori canal, dug from 1612
  • Graze your way down the 580-metre arcade of Kuromon Market
  • Visit Shitenno-ji, founded by Prince Shotoku in 593 (full day)
  • Eat takoyaki and okonomiyaki in the city they come from
