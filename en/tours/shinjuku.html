Overview

The busiest station, and the quiet around it

Shinjuku Station holds the Guinness World Record for the world's busiest station: an average of 2,704,703 people a day in 2022. I'll meet you at a subway exit instead, because the station is easy to get lost in.

The Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building has free observation rooms on the 45th floor, 202 metres up. From December to February, Mt Fuji shows on many days.

Shinjuku Gyoen started as land Tokugawa Ieyasu gave to Naito Kiyonari in 1590. It became an imperial garden and was finished in 1906 to a design by the Frenchman Henri Martinet. It opened to the public on 21 May 1949. Japan's first home-grown strawberry, the Fukuba strawberry, was raised in its greenhouse.

Good to know

  • The observation rooms can close suddenly in bad weather, and cloud can hide the view. The south room closes on the first and third Tuesday of each month.
  • Shinjuku Gyoen gets very crowded in cherry blossom season, and weekend entry may need a reservation then.
  • The bar streets around Shinjuku aren't on my routes. We go for the views, the shrine and the garden.
  • I look after the day itself. Train tickets, hotels and any entry tickets you want to book ahead are yours to arrange. I'll tell you exactly where and when to meet me.
  • We get around by train, bus and on foot, the same way people here do. I don't drive guests, and I'll show you how the ticket machines work as we go.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Where does the name Shinjuku come from?

From Naito-Shinjuku, a post town on the Koshu Kaido highway set up on part of the Naito family's land.

Why did Hanazono Shrine hold theatre shows?

Each time fire destroyed it, the shrine put up a theatre in its grounds to raise money to rebuild.
